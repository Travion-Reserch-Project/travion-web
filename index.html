Literature Survey

AI-driven tourism intelligence for smarter, safer travel in Sri Lanka

01 Smart Transportation Recommendation System

Traditional transport recommenders such as Google Maps [1] and Moovit [2] provide route planning using static schedules and traffic data. However, they lack contextual adaptation for developing-country transport ecosystems such as Sri Lanka's mix of trains, buses, tuk-tuks and private hires. Recent work on multi-modal transit optimization [3] introduces graph-neural-network-based routing that accounts for real-time delays and crowd density. Study [4] proposes a reinforcement-learning fare predictor for informal transport in Southeast Asia, achieving 91 % accuracy on tuk-tuk pricing but limited to Bangkok only. A tourism-specific recommender [5] integrates weather and cultural-event calendars into route scoring, yet still relies on pre-computed timetables rather than live feeds. Existing systems fail to combine real-time multi-modal data with traveller preference learning and fair-fare prediction in a single Sri Lanka-focused module.

02 RAG-Based Agentic Tour Planning Chatbot

Conversational AI for tourism has evolved from rule-based chatbots [6] to large-language-model (LLM) assistants. ChatGPT-based travel planners [7] generate plausible itineraries but suffer from hallucinated facts and stale knowledge. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) [8] mitigates this by grounding LLM outputs in a curated vector knowledge base, dramatically improving factual accuracy. Agentic LLM frameworks such as LangGraph [9] and AutoGen [10] enable multi-step tool-use workflows where the model autonomously searches, books and re-plans. However, no existing system combines RAG with agentic tool-use specifically for Sri Lankan tourism — a domain that requires local cultural nuance, Sinhala/Tamil language support and integration with fragmented local booking APIs.



References

03 Smart Safety Alert System

Tourist safety systems range from government travel-advisory portals [11] to crowd-sourced incident apps like Citizen [12]. Geofencing-based alert platforms [13] trigger notifications when users enter predefined risk zones, while weather-event detection models [14] use LSTM networks to forecast flash floods with 87 % recall. Wildlife-proximity warning systems using IoT sensors have been deployed in African safari parks [15] but remain absent in Sri Lankan reserves such as Yala and Wilpattu, where elephant-human encounters are frequent. Current solutions are siloed: weather, wildlife and crime alerts exist on separate platforms with no unified real-time pipeline or offline fallback for low-connectivity wilderness areas.



References


[11] U.S. Department of State, "Travel Advisories," [Online]. Available: https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories.

[15] J. Kamau, T. Otieno and L. Mwangi, "IoT-Enabled Wildlife Proximity Warning System for Safari Parks," Sensors, vol. 22, no. 12, 2022.

04 Smart High-UV Health Care System

UV exposure monitoring has been explored through wearable sensors [16] and smartphone-based UV index estimators [17]. The WHO Global Solar UV Index [18] provides standardised daily readings but lacks personalised guidance based on skin type, altitude and activity duration. A dermatology-focused app [19] uses the Fitzpatrick scale and satellite UV data to recommend sunscreen SPF levels, achieving 94 % user satisfaction in Australian trials. However, it does not integrate hydration tracking or heatstroke risk assessment, which are critical in tropical climates. Study [20] introduces a multi-sensor fusion approach combining UV, temperature and humidity for real-time heat-stress alerts in outdoor workers but has not been adapted for tourism contexts. No existing system delivers holistic UV-plus-heat health guidance personalised to travellers in Sri Lanka's diverse climate zones — from humid coastal beaches to cool hill-country elevations.



References


[16] L. Gao, Y. Zhang and H. Kim, "Wearable UV Dosimeters: A Systematic Review," Photochemistry and Photobiology, vol. 98, no. 3, pp. 512–525, 2022.

[17] D. Banerjee and P. Agrawal, "Smartphone-Based Real-Time UV Index Estimation Using Camera Sensors," IEEE Sensors Journal, vol. 21, no. 15, 2021.

Research Gaps

Key limitations identified across existing tourism technology and AI-driven travel platforms

Lack of Context-Aware Multi-Modal Transport

Current route planners rely on static schedules and ignore the informal transit systems, fair-fare prediction and real-time crowd/weather conditions unique to Sri Lanka's transport landscape.

No Agentic RAG Chatbot for Sri Lankan Tourism

Existing LLM travel assistants hallucinate facts and lack grounding in local Sri Lankan knowledge. No system combines RAG retrieval with autonomous tool-use agents for booking and re-planning.

Fragmented Safety Information for Tourists

Weather, wildlife and terrain hazards are scattered across siloed platforms. No unified real-time safety pipeline with offline fallback exists for travellers in Sri Lanka's remote regions.

Absence of Personalised UV & Heat Health Guidance

No tourism app delivers holistic sun-exposure and heatstroke guidance personalised to skin type, altitude and activity — critical in Sri Lanka's tropical climate zones from coast to hill country.

Research Problem & Solution

From Problem to Solution: Advancing AI-Powered Travel Intelligence for Sri Lanka

Problem

How can an AI-powered mobile platform be developed to provide unified, context-aware travel intelligence for tourists visiting Sri Lanka — specifically addressing smart multi-modal transportation recommendations, agentic RAG-based trip planning, real-time safety alerts across diverse terrains, and personalised UV/heat health guidance — when current solutions offer fragmented information, hallucinated recommendations and no real-time awareness of local transport, safety or environmental hazards?


Proposed Solution

To address the identified gaps, the proposed solution is Travion — a unified AI-powered mobile platform that integrates four intelligent modules into a single Sri Lanka-focused travel companion. It employs a smart transportation recommendation engine that fuses real-time transit feeds, traffic, weather and user preferences to optimise multi-modal routes with fair-fare prediction for tuk-tuks, buses and trains.


A RAG-based agentic chatbot, grounded in a curated vector knowledge base of Sri Lankan destinations, autonomously plans multi-day itineraries through natural conversation. The smart safety alert module unifies weather, wildlife and community-sourced hazard data into a real-time geofenced notification pipeline with offline support. Finally, the UV health care system personalises sun-exposure guidance, hydration reminders and heatstroke warnings using satellite UV indices, altitude data and the traveller's skin profile.

Research Objectives

Four targeted objectives powering every Travion feature

1

Smart multi-modal transportation recommendations for Sri Lankan travel

This objective aims to develop a context-aware transport recommendation engine that fuses real-time transit data, traffic conditions, weather and traveller preferences to suggest optimal multi-modal routes across Sri Lanka. The system predicts fair tuk-tuk fares, train availability and intercity bus schedules, continuously re-planning when conditions change.



RAG-based agentic chatbot for autonomous tour planning

This objective focuses on building a conversational AI agent backed by a curated Sri Lankan travel knowledge base using Retrieval-Augmented Generation. The chatbot autonomously plans multi-day itineraries, books activities, answers cultural queries and iteratively refines plans as the traveller's interests evolve — all through natural dialogue.

Real-time smart safety alert system for diverse terrains

This objective targets developing a unified safety pipeline that combines geofenced risk zones, live weather warnings, wildlife proximity alerts and community-sourced incident reports. The methodology involves developing an AI-driven mobile platform called "Travion," which uses multi-modal AI technologies to plan, guide and protect tourists exploring Sri Lanka. A Flutter front-end communicates with a FastAPI back-end that orchestrates LLM agents, vector databases of Sri Lankan points of interest, real-time transit and weather APIs, and on-device sensors for location and UV estimation. All modules share a common data layer cached for offline use in low-signal zones.


For each component, the system employs specialised AI modules:


  • (1) Smart Transport uses graph-based route optimisation over live transit feeds, reinforcement-learning fare prediction and predictive ETA models for multi-modal routing.


  • (2) The RAG Chatbot leverages LLM agents with a Pinecone vector DB, LangChain tool-use and autonomous re-planning workflows to generate and refine multi-day itineraries.


  • (3) Safety Alerts combine geofencing, weather APIs, wildlife proximity detection and community-sourced incident reports via a real-time event streaming pipeline with offline fallback.


  • (4) The UV Health module fuses satellite UV indices with altitude, humidity and Fitzpatrick skin-type profiles to generate personalised sun-exposure plans and heatstroke warnings.
