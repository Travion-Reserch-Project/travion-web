About Travion

Mass-market travel apps don't speak Sri Lanka.

They show destinations and hotel listings, but they often miss real traveler needs such as safe routes, weather conditions, transport tips, language barriers, hidden attractions, crowd levels, and personalized plans. Travel Companion is built specifically to help visitors explore Sri Lanka smarter, safer, and with confidence through AI-powered guidance.

The Problem

Generic, globalised, context-blind.

Existing travel platforms from major booking aggregators to AI chatbots treat Sri Lanka as a line item in a global index. They surface Western-leaning hotels, miss the rhythm of Buddhist poya days, ignore monsoon micro-climates, and provide no localised safety information for solo travellers, women, or trekkers.

Local travellers and inbound tourists alike report the same frustration: the island deserves better storytelling and smarter logistics.

Our Solution

A travel mind that grew up here.

Travion is a unified AI-powered mobile platform integrating four intelligent modules into a single Sri Lanka-focused travel companion. It fuses real-time transit, traffic, weather and user preferences to optimise multi-modal routes with fair-fare prediction for tuk-tuks, buses and trains.

A RAG-based agentic chatbot grounded in a curated vector knowledge base of Sri Lankan destinations autonomously plans multi-day itineraries. A smart safety module uses AI-driven risk prediction based on historical crime data, crowdsourced incident reports, and geospatial context to generate real-time alerts, live risk heatmaps, and nearby police assistance recommendations for tourists, while the UV health system personalises sun-exposure and heatstroke guidance using satellite data, altitude and skin profile.

Literature Review

The foundation of our research.

A rigorous survey of AI tourism, Sri Lankan travel behaviour, and the gaps that existing platforms leave unanswered.

01

Smart Transportation Recommendation System

Traditional transport recommenders such as Google Maps [1] and Moovit [2] provide route planning using static schedules and traffic data. However, they lack contextual adaptation for developing-country transport ecosystems such as Sri Lanka's mix of trains, buses, tuk-tuks and private hires. Recent work on multi-modal transit optimization [3] introduces graph-neural-network-based routing that accounts for real-time delays and crowd density. Study [4] proposes a reinforcement-learning fare predictor for informal transport in Southeast Asia, achieving 91 % accuracy on tuk-tuk pricing but limited to Bangkok only. A tourism-specific recommender [5] integrates weather and cultural-event calendars into route scoring, yet still relies on pre-computed timetables rather than live feeds. Existing systems fail to combine real-time multi-modal data with traveller preference learning and fair-fare prediction in a single Sri Lanka-focused module.

References
  1. Google LLC, "Google Maps Platform - Routes API," [Online]. Available: https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/routes.
  2. Moovit Inc., "Moovit - Smart Public Transit App," [Online]. Available: https://moovit.com.
  3. Z. Wang, Y. Li and K. Chen, "GNN-Transit: Graph Neural Network Based Multi-Modal Route Optimization," IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems, vol. 24, no. 8, pp. 1–12, 2023.
  4. P. Chaikaew and S. Nontapa, "Reinforcement Learning-Based Fare Prediction for Informal Transit in Southeast Asia," Proceedings of the ACM SIGSPATIAL, 2022, pp. 45–54.
  5. A. Perera, D. Jayawardena and R. Gunawardena, "Context-Aware Tourist Route Recommender with Weather and Event Integration," 2023 International Conference on Advances in Computing (ICAC), pp. 112–118.
02

RAG-Based Agentic Tour Planning Chatbot

Conversational AI for tourism has evolved from rule-based chatbots [6] to large-language-model (LLM) assistants. ChatGPT-based travel planners [7] generate plausible itineraries but suffer from hallucinated facts and stale knowledge. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) [8] mitigates this by grounding LLM outputs in a curated vector knowledge base, dramatically improving factual accuracy. Agentic LLM frameworks such as LangGraph [9] and AutoGen [10] enable multi-step tool-use workflows where the model autonomously searches, books and re-plans. However, no existing system combines RAG with agentic tool-use specifically for Sri Lankan tourism - a domain that requires local cultural nuance, Sinhala/Tamil language support and integration with fragmented local booking APIs.

References
  1. B. Abu Shawar and E. Atwell, "Chatbots: Are They Really Useful?," Journal for Language Technology and Computational Linguistics, vol. 22, no. 1, pp. 29–49, 2007.
  2. S. Mao, D. Mathew and R. Joshi, "ChatTravel: LLM-Powered Itinerary Generation — Promises and Pitfalls," ACM Conference on AI for Tourism, 2024, pp. 1–10.
  3. P. Lewis et al., "Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks," Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), vol. 33, 2020.

  5. Q. Wu et al., "AutoGen: Enabling Next-Gen LLM Applications via Multi-Agent Conversation," arXiv preprint arXiv:2308.08155, 2023.
03

AI-Driven Safety Risk Prediction Systems

Tourist safety systems range from government travel-advisory portals [11] to crowd-sourced incident apps like Citizen [12]. Geofencing-based alert platforms [13] trigger notifications when users enter predefined risk zones, while weather-event detection models [14] use LSTM networks to forecast flash floods with 87 % recall. Wildlife-proximity warning systems using IoT sensors have been deployed in African safari parks [15] but remain absent in Sri Lankan reserves such as Yala and Wilpattu, where elephant-human encounters are frequent. Current solutions are siloed: weather, wildlife and crime alerts exist on separate platforms with no unified real-time pipeline or offline fallback for low-connectivity wilderness areas.

References
  1. U.S. Department of State, "Travel Advisories," [Online]. Available: https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories.
  2. Citizen App Inc., "Citizen — Connect and Stay Safe," [Online]. Available: https://citizen.com.
  3. M. Al-Turjman, "Geofencing-Based Safety Alert Framework for Smart Tourism," IEEE Internet of Things Journal, vol. 10, no. 4, 2023.
  4. R. Fernando and K. Silva, "LSTM-Based Flash Flood Prediction for Tropical River Basins," 2022 International Conference on Data Science (ICDS), pp. 201–207.
  5. J. Kamau, T. Otieno and L. Mwangi, "IoT-Enabled Wildlife Proximity Warning System for Safari Parks," Sensors, vol. 22, no. 12, 2022.
04

Smart UV & Weather Safety Advisor

UV exposure monitoring has been explored through wearable sensors [16] and smartphone-based UV index estimators [17]. The WHO Global Solar UV Index [18] provides standardised daily readings but lacks personalised guidance based on skin type, altitude and activity duration. A dermatology-focused app [19] uses the Fitzpatrick scale and satellite UV data to recommend sunscreen SPF levels, achieving 94 % user satisfaction in Australian trials. However, it does not integrate hydration tracking or heatstroke risk assessment, which are critical in tropical climates. Study [20] introduces a multi-sensor fusion approach combining UV, temperature and humidity for real-time heat-stress alerts in outdoor workers but has not been adapted for tourism contexts. No existing system delivers holistic UV-plus-heat health guidance personalised to travellers in Sri Lanka's diverse climate zones — from humid coastal beaches to cool hill-country elevations.

References
  1. L. Gao, Y. Zhang and H. Kim, "Wearable UV Dosimeters: A Systematic Review," Photochemistry and Photobiology, vol. 98, no. 3, pp. 512–525, 2022.
  2. D. Banerjee and P. Agrawal, "Smartphone-Based Real-Time UV Index Estimation Using Camera Sensors," IEEE Sensors Journal, vol. 21, no. 15, 2021.
  3. World Health Organization, "Global Solar UV Index: A Practical Guide," WHO/SDE/OEH/02.2, 2002.
  4. K. Thompson, M. Williams and R. Lee, "SunSafe: Personalised UV Protection App Using Fitzpatrick Skin Typing and Satellite Data," Australasian Journal of Dermatology, vol. 64, no. 2, 2023.
  5. H. Chen, J. Liu and W. Zhao, "Multi-Sensor Fusion for Real-Time Heat Stress Assessment in Outdoor Workers," Sensors, vol. 23, no. 8, 2023.
Research Gaps

Four gaps, one unified answer.

Lack of Context-Aware Multi-Modal Transport

Current route planners rely on static schedules and ignore the informal transit systems, fair-fare prediction and real-time crowd/weather conditions unique to Sri Lanka's transport landscape.

No Agentic RAG Chatbot for Sri Lankan Tourism

Existing LLM travel assistants hallucinate facts and lack grounding in local Sri Lankan knowledge. No system combines RAG retrieval with autonomous tool-use agents for booking and re-planning.

Weak Predictive Tourist Safety Support

Most safety tools provide only reactive alerts. Predictive crime risk analysis with real-time mobile notifications for tourists is still limited.

Absence of Personalised UV & Heat Health Guidance

No tourism app delivers holistic sun-exposure and heatstroke guidance personalised to skin type, altitude and activity - critical in Sri Lanka's tropical climate zones from coast to hill country.

Our Objectives

Four pillars, one island.

Our project objectives define the full scope of Travion - from intelligent planning to safety, from discovery to sustainable travel.

01

Smart multi-modal transportation recommendations

A context-aware engine fuses real-time transit data, traffic, weather and traveller preferences to suggest optimal multi-modal routes across Sri Lanka. The system predicts fair tuk-tuk fares, train availability and intercity bus schedules, continuously re-planning when conditions change.

02

RAG-based agentic chatbot for autonomous tour planning

A conversational AI agent backed by a curated Sri Lankan travel knowledge base using Retrieval-Augmented Generation. It autonomously plans multi-day itineraries, books activities, answers cultural queries and iteratively refines plans as the traveller's interests evolve - all through natural dialogue

03

AI-Driven Safety Risk Prediction and Real-Time Alerts

Develop a smart safety module that uses historical crime data, crowdsourced incident reports, and geospatial context to predict tourist safety risks, generate real-time alerts, visualize danger zones through live heatmaps, and recommend nearby police assistance.

04

Personalized UV and heat health care for tropical travel

Personalized sun-exposure coaching using satellite UV indices, altitude, humidity and the traveller's skin profile. The system sends hydration nudges, sunscreen reminders and heatstroke warnings so visitors stay healthy under the tropical sun - from Mirissa's coast to Adam's Peak.

The methodology involves developing an AI-driven mobile platform called "Travion" that uses multi-modal AI technologies to plan, guide and protect tourists exploring Sri Lanka. A React Native front-end communicates with a FastAPI back-end that orchestrates LLM agents, vector databases of Sri Lankan points of interest, real-time transit and weather APIs, and on-device sensors for location and UV estimation.

  • 1Smart Transport uses graph-based route optimisation over live transit feeds, reinforcement-learning fare prediction and predictive ETA models for multi-modal routing.
  • 2RAG Chatbot leverages LLM agents with a Pinecone vector DB, LangChain tool-use and autonomous re-planning workflows to generate and refine multi-day itineraries.
  • 3Safety Risk Prediction Module uses historical crime records, crowdsourced reports, GPS location data, and machine learning models to predict risks, trigger alerts, and recommend nearby police stations.
  • 4UV Health fuses satellite UV indices with altitude, humidity and Fitzpatrick skin-type profiles to generate personalised sun-exposure plans and heatstroke warnings.
