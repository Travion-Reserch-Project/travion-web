About Travion

Mass-market travel apps don't speak Sri Lanka.

They list hotels and landmarks. They can't tell you that the Ella train has no toilets, that tuk-tuks in Galle use meters but in Kandy you barter, or that the 14:00 heat on Sigiriya rock is unforgiving. Travion is designed from the ground up to know.

The Problem

Generic, globalised, context-blind.

Existing travel platforms — from major booking aggregators to AI chatbots — treat Sri Lanka as a line item in a global index. They surface Western-leaning hotels, miss the rhythm of Buddhist poya days, ignore monsoon micro-climates, and provide no localised safety information for solo travellers, women, or trekkers.

Local travellers and inbound tourists alike report the same frustration: the island deserves better storytelling and smarter logistics.

Our Solution

A travel mind that grew up here.

Travion is a mobile-first, AI-powered companion trained on Sri Lanka-specific data — from Department of Meteorology feeds to Sri Lanka Tourism Promotion Bureau guidelines, from crowd-sourced tuk-tuk fare maps to UNESCO heritage guidance.

It plans intelligent day-by-day itineraries, watches the skies in real-time, recommends experiences locals actually endorse, and proactively warns you when a trail is unsafe or a train is overcrowded.

Literature Review

The foundation of our research.

A rigorous survey of AI tourism, Sri Lankan travel behaviour, and the gaps that existing platforms leave unanswered.

01

AI-driven personalisation in tourism

Recent surveys of recommendation systems in hospitality (Gretzel et al., 2020; Buhalis & Sinarta, 2019) show that contextual AI — which blends user preferences with real-time signals such as weather, crowding and transit — outperforms static curated content for traveller satisfaction. However, almost all production systems rely on aggregated global data; regional specificity is treated as an afterthought.

References
  1. Gretzel, U. et al. (2020). Smart tourism: foundations and developments. Electronic Markets.
  2. Buhalis, D. & Sinarta, Y. (2019). Real-time co-creation and nowness service. Journal of Travel & Tourism Marketing.
02

Sri Lankan tourism: opportunities & pain points

Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority (SLTDA, 2023) reports 1.48 million inbound tourists in 2023 with strong post-pandemic recovery. Jayawardena (2013) and Ranasinghe (2019) identify persistent challenges: information asymmetry between operators and travellers, language barriers outside Colombo, and inconsistent safety communication. No existing digital product addresses these at national scale.

References
  1. SLTDA Annual Statistical Report 2023.
  2. Jayawardena, C. (2013). Innovative solutions for future tourism development. Worldwide Hospitality Review.
  3. Ranasinghe, R. (2019). Destination branding of Sri Lanka post-conflict. Anatolia.
03

Conversational AI & natural-language trip planning

Advances in large language models (Brown et al., 2020; OpenAI, 2023) enable natural dialogue-based travel planning. Ivanov & Webster (2021) document promising results for chatbot-driven concierge services, but highlight that domain-specific fine-tuning and retrieval augmentation are essential for factual accuracy in niche geographies.

References
  1. Brown, T. et al. (2020). Language Models are Few-Shot Learners. NeurIPS.
  2. Ivanov, S. & Webster, C. (2021). Robots, Artificial Intelligence and Service Automation in Travel. Emerald.
04

Safety, sustainability & responsible travel tech

Research on responsible tourism (Font & McCabe, 2017) emphasises the role of digital platforms in steering travellers towards sustainable choices. UNWTO (2022) calls for tech solutions that support dispersal beyond over-touristed hotspots — critical in Sri Lanka, where Sigiriya and Ella face peak-season congestion while the North and East remain underserved.

References
  1. Font, X. & McCabe, S. (2017). Sustainability & marketing in tourism. Journal of Sustainable Tourism.
  2. UNWTO (2022). Tourism for Inclusive Growth.
Research Gaps

Localisation deficit

Almost no AI travel product supports Sinhala or Tamil natively. Travion is trilingual (EN · SI · TA) with code-switching awareness.

Our Objectives

Five pillars, one island.

Our project objectives define the full scope of Travion — from intelligent planning to safety, from discovery to sustainable travel.

01

Intelligent trip planning

Generate personalised, day-by-day itineraries that respect weather, opening hours, pace preferences and cultural calendars — built in under 30 seconds.

02

Real-time safety & weather

Integrate live feeds from the Sri Lanka Department of Meteorology, police advisories and wildlife services to alert travellers of hazards in their current zone.

03

Trilingual local insight

Deliver natural-language travel conversations in English, Sinhala and Tamil — with cultural context most AI products miss.

04

Transparent local economies

Surface locally-owned homestays, tuk-tuk co-operatives and small restaurants — distributing tourist spend beyond chain hotels.

05

Sustainable dispersal

Nudge travellers towards under-visited regions in the North and East, reducing congestion at flagship sites and broadening economic impact.

Travion is built in an iterative, user-centred loop. We combine design thinking, on-the-ground user research in Sri Lanka, and modern ML engineering.

  • 1Discover. Ethnographic interviews with 40 travellers and 20 local operators across the Cultural Triangle, South Coast, Hill Country and North.
  • 2Define. Journey-map pain points, extract core jobs-to-be-done, align with SLTDA priorities.
  • 3Design. Low-fi → high-fi prototypes in Figma, tested with real travellers in-situ.
  • 4Develop. Flutter mobile + FastAPI backend + fine-tuned LLM with RAG over a curated Sri Lanka corpus.
  • 5Deliver. Beta pilots in Colombo & Kandy, iterate quarterly with tourism-board feedback.
